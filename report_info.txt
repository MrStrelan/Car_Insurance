(A) ClaimRate → ClaimNb (0.26)
This is expected since ClaimRate = ClaimNb / Exposure.
Ignore for model insight.
(B) Exposure (0.07)
Policies that are insured longer have more chance to claim → correct & expected.
Not a true predictor, but an offset factor.
(C) BonusMalus (0.05)
This is the most meaningful predictor in the dataset.
BonusMalus summarizes historical driving behavior:
Low Bonus → safe driver
High Bonus → risky driver
Even a small correlation is meaningful due to data imbalance.
(D) VehAge (0.02)
Older vehicles show slightly more claims.
Weak linearly, but ML models normally find more in nonlinear splits.
(E) DrivAge (0.01)
Claims often have a U-shape with driver age:
young drivers → risky
middle age → safest
elderly → rising risk again
Linear correlation cannot capture a U-shape, so this low number is expected.
(F) Density (0.01)
Urban areas → more traffic → more accidents.
But again, relationship is nonlinear, so correlation underestimates importance.
VehPower (0.0046)
Barely correlates linearly, but tree-based models often find it important.
Possible explanation: power interacts with driver profile - bonus/malus.

We have done PCA on the cleaned training dataset:
Preprocessing:
Selected the numerical features 'Exposure', 'VehPower', 'VehAge', 'DrivAge', 'BonusMalus', 'Density'.
Log Transformed the highly skewed features: BonusMalus, Density.
Applied feature scaling: Standarization (x - mean)/ std for each numeric feature.

PCA:
2 principal components, total variance of 47%, meaning the 2D PCA captures 47% of the total dataset.
PC1 is 27%, PC2 is 20%.
A third component brings the total up to 64% variance in the whole dataset, with PC3 being 16%.
Pca is very useful when exploring structure in the dataset.

K-Means clustering:
Done both on PCA, and separately on the training dataset.
Used only numerical columns.
With k=4 clusters:
Cluster 3 has highest BonusMalus, with 83.57.
Cluster 2 has highest Driver Age, with 51.20.
Cluster 0 and 2 have similar Claim Frequncies, 0.152, and 0.154 respectively.
Cluster 1 has highest exposure, 0.90. etc.

Clustering plots:
Cluster 2 shows the highest claim frequency, while cluster 1 is lowest, meaning that cluster 2 contains higher-risk policies.
Cluster 2 shows younger drivers, newer vehicles.
Cluster 1 shows older drivers, older cars.
Clusters with higher BonusMalus and higher density tend to show elevated claim frequencies, indicating that urban, high-penalty drivers are riskier.
